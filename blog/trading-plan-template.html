Trading Plan Template: Write It Once, Follow It Forever

Ask a losing trader for their plan and you'll get a vibe. Ask a consistently profitable one and you'll get a document. The difference isn't intelligence; it's that a written plan removes decisions from the moment of maximum emotion. Build yours in six sections:

1. Market and timeframe

Which instruments, which session, which chart timeframe. Vague scope produces random trades.

2. Setup definition

Your exact entry conditions, written so a stranger could take the trade. "Oversold RSI" is a mood; "RSI <30 plus higher-timeframe support confluence plus volume spike" is a setup.

3. Exit rules before entry

Stop distance, target logic (fixed R multiple or structure), and what invalidates the idea mid-trade. Exits decided during a trade are decisions made by adrenaline.

4. Position sizing formula

Risk per trade as % of account (most serious retail traders land 0.5–1%), position size derived from stop distance. This single rule prevents the account-ending trade.

5. Daily loss limit

A hard number that ends your day when hit. Revenge trades happen inside the gap between "I'm down" and "I'm done."

6. Review cadence

Weekly journal review: which setups paid, which leaked, did rules get broken and why. The plan is a living document reviewed against data, not vibes.

FAQ

Does a trading plan actually improve results? It cannot fix a negative-edge strategy, but it reliably stops the three account killers: oversizing, revenge trading, and moving stops.

Paper trading or real money first? Paper to validate mechanics, small size to validate psychology. Both matter; only the second is optional-proof.
