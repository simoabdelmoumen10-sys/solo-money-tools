Freelance Rate Calculator: The Formula Behind Your Number

"What should I charge?" has an exact answer, and it starts with a fact most freelancers ignore: you can only bill roughly 60% of your working hours. The rest goes to sales, admin, learning, and the inbox. Rate math that ignores this underprices you by ~40%. The formula:

Step 1: Target income

Salary you'd want + self-employment tax cushion (20–30%) + benefits you must self-fund (health, retirement). This is your gross revenue target, not your dream net.

Step 2: Billable reality check

Working hours × 0.6. A 40-hour week yields ~24 billable hours. Annualize honestly: minus vacation, sick days, and the December drought.

Step 3: Overhead per hour

Software, insurance, hardware, coworking, accountant — total annual, divided by billable hours. Typically adds $5–15/hour.

Step 4: The number

(Target gross + overhead) ÷ billable hours = minimum hourly rate

Then round UP to a clean figure. Clean numbers signal confidence and simplify quoting.

Beyond hourly

Once you know your floor, price per project and value wherever possible: hourly rates punish you for getting faster. Your calculated rate is a foundation — quotes built on packages anchored to it will always clear it.

FAQ

What's a realistic first-year freelance rate? For US generalists in 2026, $50–75/hr clears costs comfortably once the formula includes taxes and the 60% billable factor; specialists command more.

When should I recalculate? Every January and after any big expense change. Rates only ratchet up.
